How to play

Bid on dice you can't see.

01

Your cup

You and Wren each get five dice under a cup. You see your own five. Nobody sees hers, not even the game.

02

Bid

Claim what all ten dice hold. "Four 3s" means at least four 3s between both cups. Ones are wild unless ones are bid. Each bid must go higher: more dice, or the same number on a higher face.

03

Or call it a lie

The game counts all ten dice blind and says one word. HELD: the bidder takes the round. LIAR: the caller does. Two rounds win the match.

04

Read the table

Count your dice that match, ones included, and expect her to hold one or two more. A bid that needs more than that is probably a lie. And watch her hands.

The house player

Wren

Plays the river taverns. Never looks at your cup, because she can't.

  • Her habit. She always opens on the face she holds most.
  • Her tell. Watch her hands.
  • Her seat. She never sends a transaction. She signs each move and your wallet carries it, so she plays without ever holding gas.
  • Her limits. The Zama ACL lets her decrypt her own five dice and nothing else, so she can't cheat any more than you can.

How it stays fair

Nobody sees the dice. Not even the game.

01

Rolled encrypted

Each die is drawn on chain from Zama's encrypted randomness. No server, no player and no developer picks a value or can read one.

02

Counted blind

When a bid is called, the contract counts all ten dice for that face, ones wild, entirely on ciphertexts, and compares the count with the bid.

03

One bit out

Only that answer is made public: the bid held, or it was a lie. Zama's KMS signs it and the contract checks the proof. The cups stay sealed forever.

The obvious question

Why not commit-reveal?

Commit-reveal settles a call by making both players open their cups. That breaks liar's dice three ways.

  • The loser can walk. Whoever sees they are losing just never reveals. Here nobody reveals anything: the count runs on ciphertexts and anyone can settle it.
  • The bluff dies with the reveal. Once your cup is open, everyone knows how you bid with it. Here your dice are never shown, so your style stays yours.
  • Someone still rolls the dice. Commitments hide values a player chose. Here the dice come from encrypted on-chain randomness that no one chose.
